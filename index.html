Key assumptions

How the numbers above are worked out.

Electric Car Discount (FBT)

Battery-electric and hydrogen cars below the fuel-efficient LCT threshold ($91,661 in 2026–27) are FBT-exempt. Leases committed before 1 April 2027 keep this for their term. Plug-in hybrids only qualify for arrangements made before 1 April 2025.

Proposed changes (not yet law)

Announced in the 2026–27 Budget, draft legislation released: from 1 April 2027 the full exemption applies only to EVs costing $75,000 or less; dearer EVs get a 25% discount (15% statutory fraction). From 1 April 2029 all eligible EVs get the 25% discount.

FBT & post-tax contribution

Where FBT applies, the calculator assumes you make a post-tax employee contribution (ECM) equal to the taxable value (base value × statutory fraction), so your employer pays no FBT. This is how most novated leases are structured.

GST

Your employer claims the GST on the car, capped at 1/11 of the 2026–27 car limit ($69,883, so up to $6,353), and on running costs except registration.

Tax rates

2026–27 resident rates (15% / 30% / 37% / 45%), the low income tax offset and the 2% Medicare levy with the low-income reduction (latest published thresholds, 2025–26). Excludes HELP, the $1,000 standard deduction and other offsets.

Reportable fringe benefits

Even an FBT-exempt EV is reported on your income statement (20% statutory fraction, grossed up by 1.8868). It isn't taxed, but counts for HELP repayments, Medicare levy surcharge and some government payments.

Residual & car loan

Residual uses ATO minimum percentages and is due at lease end (pay it, refinance or sell). The car loan comparison finances the full price at the same rate from after-tax pay, with no residual.

Prices

Preset prices are list prices before on-road costs, checked November 2026. The LCT and $75,000 tests use the car's value, not on-road costs, so compare your quote carefully.
